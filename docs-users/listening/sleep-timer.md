The sleep timer stops the book for you, so you can listen yourself to sleep without losing your place. Tap the **moon** icon in the player (or in the player bar on a tablet or computer) to open it. In the web player, the **Z** key opens it too.

## Setting a timer

The sleep timer offers:

- **Minutes** - 5, 10, 15, 30, 45 or 60.
- **End of chapter** - stops when the current chapter ends, with how long that is from now ("in 12m"). For a book without chapters it reads **End of book** instead.
- **Or stop after** - for a book with real chapters, a list of this chapter and the next three, each with the clock time it ends and how long until then: *"3 chapters · The Shattered Plains · ends 23:41 · 1h 4m"*. Pick how many chapters you think you'll last.

Tap one and the timer starts and the sheet closes. All the times are real time at your current speed: at 2×, a chapter ends twice as soon.

## While a timer runs

The countdown shows on the moon icon and on the cover. Open the sleep timer again and the top of it says what's set - *"Sleep timer on"*, *"Stopping at the end of this chapter"*, *"Stopping after 3 chapters"* (which counts down as chapters go by) or *"Stopping at the end of the book"* - with the time left and a **Turn off** button. Pick a different option to replace the timer.

When the timer fires, playback simply pauses - nothing is lost.

A timer belongs to the book you set it on. Start a different book, or finish the one you're on, and the timer goes with it instead of following you to the next book. And if a timer reaches its point when you'd already paused by hand, it just ends quietly: there was nothing left to stop.

## Pausing with a timer running

**A minutes timer pauses with the book.** Pause, and the countdown stops where it is; press play and it carries on from there. Thirty minutes means thirty minutes of *listening*, not thirty minutes on the clock - so answering the door, taking a call, or pausing with a headphone button without ever looking at the screen can't quietly use up a timer you were relying on. It works the same way if you set a timer before you press play: the countdown starts when the audio does.

**Come back after more than 20 minutes and the timer starts again at its full length.** A break that long is a new sitting rather than an interruption, and the alternative is worse: a timer frozen with three minutes left, forgotten about, stopping you three minutes into the next evening's listening. Under 20 minutes it simply picks up where it left off. And after more than **two hours** away, the timer is simply over: picking the book up the next day doesn't bring last night's timer back. There's nothing to configure.

A **chapter** timer needs none of this: it stops at a place in the book, not after an amount of time, so it waits exactly where you left it however long you're away, and still stops at the chapter you chose.

## The last 30 seconds, and how to keep listening

Every timer has a **final 30 seconds** that are your cue it's about to stop - and your chance to carry on if you're still awake. A card appears over the player with a ring that empties as the seconds run out:

- A **minutes** timer stops at an arbitrary point mid-chapter, so it doesn't cut the book off mid-word: the audio **fades out gently** over those 30 seconds. The card says *"Fading out in 24 s"*.
- A **chapter** timer plays those 30 seconds at **full volume** and stops at the boundary. Those closing words are the ones you stayed awake for, and the chapter ending is its own signal that the book is about to stop. The card says *"Stopping in 24 s"*.

The card stays for **30 seconds after playback has paused** too (*"Paused by the sleep timer"*). Until it goes, one gesture keeps you going:

- **Tap Keep listening** - on the card, or in the sleep timer. This works everywhere.
- **Shake your phone** - in the iOS and Android apps (below).

Either one brings the volume straight back up (if it had started to fade) and **starts the timer again**: a 30-minute timer becomes a fresh 30 minutes, and a chapter timer moves on to the end of the **next** chapter - just one more, even if you'd asked to stop after three, since a shake that late means "a little more". If playback had already stopped, it starts playing again too - so you never have to unlock the phone to rescue a book you were still listening to.

Once those 30 seconds are up, the timer is finished and the book stays paused where it was.

:::note
Safari on iPhone and iPad doesn't let a web page change its own volume, so in the **web player on those devices** a minutes timer's last 30 seconds don't audibly fade. Everything else works as described, including the card and **Keep listening**. (A chapter timer never fades anywhere, so it behaves identically on every platform.)
:::

### Shake to extend

In the iOS and Android apps, a shake of the phone in those two windows does the same as **Keep listening**. The phone only listens for a shake then, so shaking it at any other time does nothing, and a bump in your pocket can't disturb a running timer.

Two settings, in the sleep timer itself and in [Settings](account.md#sleep-timer):

- **Shake to extend** - On out of the box. Turn it off if you'd rather only ever use the button.
- **Shake sensitivity** - **Low**, **Medium** (the starting point) or **High**: how hard the shake has to be. Choose **Low** if a restless partner or a bump of the mattress keeps the book going; choose **High** if a sleepy flick of the wrist isn't being noticed.

The web player can't feel the phone move, so there it says *"Not available in the browser"* and **Keep listening** is the way to carry on.

## If you fell asleep

When the timer stopped a book you were playing and nobody kept it going in those last 30 seconds - no shake, no tap, no pressing play, no moving around the book - AudioSilo assumes you fell asleep, and does two things:

- **A "Fell asleep" bookmark** goes on the book where the timer stopped it, so you can see where the night ended. It's saved to your account like any other bookmark (it's simply skipped if the server can't be reached at that moment).
- **The next time you play the book** on that device, a note offers to take you back: *"You drifted off around 23:41. Jump back 4 minutes?"* Tap **Jump back** to return to the last moment you touched the player - played, paused, skipped, set the timer - which is the last moment you were provably awake.

The offer comes once, within a day and a half, and only when it makes sense: when the gap is between a minute and an hour, and when you start again near where the timer stopped (if you've already moved on, it stays quiet).

## Starting a timer automatically at night

If you listen yourself to sleep most nights, you can have AudioSilo set the timer for you instead of remembering to. Turn on **Auto sleep timer** - in the sleep timer itself, or in [Settings](account.md#sleep-timer), where you also choose the hours it applies to (10:00 PM to 6:00 AM out of the box) and what kind of timer it sets. Any book you start inside that window gets one automatically.

It's deliberately unobtrusive: never more than one timer at a time, and never on top of a timer you set yourself.

**If a timer runs out and you press play again inside the window, you get a fresh one.** Starting the book again at 3:00 AM is you saying you're still listening, so the night's remaining hours are covered too rather than leaving you unprotected after the first timer.

**Turn a timer off and that's final.** Nothing will set another one for that book for the rest of the session (until you next start the app), because a timer you've just dismissed coming quietly back is the last thing you want. That holds for the timers you set by hand as well: turning off your own keeps the automatic one away too, while letting your own run out doesn't.
