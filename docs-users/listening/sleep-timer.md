---
title: Set a sleep timer
description: Stop the book after some minutes or chapters, keep it going with a shake, and find where you drifted off.
---

The sleep timer stops the book for you, so you can fall asleep listening without losing your place. Tap the sleep button (an alarm clock) in the full player or on the player bar to open it. In the web player, the **Z** key opens it too.

## Setting a timer

![The sleep timer sheet on a phone: minute tiles, End of chapter and the Or stop after list](/img/screenshots/web-player/phone-sleep-sheet.png)

Pick one of these to start the timer:

- **Minutes**: 5, 10, 15, 30, 45 or 60.
- **End of chapter**: stops when the current chapter ends. A book without chapter marks uses its files or [virtual chapters](playback.md#books-without-chapters).
- **Or stop after**: this chapter and up to three more, each with the clock time it ends.

The times are real time at your current speed.

## While a timer runs

The countdown shows on the sleep button, and in the mini player on a phone. To change the timer, open it again: **Turn off** stops it, or pick another option to replace it. When the timer runs out, the book pauses.

A minutes timer pauses with the book, so thirty minutes means thirty minutes of listening. Come back after more than 20 minutes and it starts again at its full length. After more than two hours, it's over. A chapter timer waits however long you're away.

A timer belongs to its book: start another book and the timer goes away. If a book ends while its timer is running, the next book doesn't [start by itself](end-of-book.md#playing-on-automatically).

### On the lock screen (iPhone)

In the iPhone app, a running timer also shows on the lock screen as a Live Activity, and in the Dynamic Island on iPhones that have one. It shows the book, the chapter and the countdown. Tap it to open the player.

## The last 30 seconds

In a timer's last 30 seconds, a card appears with a ring that empties:

- A **minutes** timer fades the book out.
- A **chapter** timer plays at full volume and stops at the chapter's end.

The card stays for 30 seconds after the book pauses. Until it goes, tap **Keep listening** or [shake your phone](#shake-to-extend) to start the timer again. You get the same minutes again, or one more chapter.

:::note
Safari on iPhone and iPad doesn't let a web page change its volume. In the web player there, a minutes timer stops without fading out.
:::

### Shake to extend

In the iOS and Android apps, a shake during those last seconds does the same as **Keep listening**. At any other time, a shake does nothing. You'll find two settings in the sleep timer and in [Settings](settings.md#sleep):

- **Shake to extend**, on unless you turn it off.
- **Shake sensitivity**: **Low**, **Medium** or **High**. Choose **Low** if bumps in bed keep the book going.

The web player can't feel the phone move, so it doesn't offer this.

## If you fell asleep

When the timer stops a book and nobody kept it going, AudioSilo assumes you fell asleep:

- A [**Fell asleep** bookmark](bookmarks-and-notes.md#fell-asleep) goes where the timer stopped the book.
- If you play the book again on that device soon after, near where it stopped, a note offers once to take you back: *"You drifted off around 23:41. Jump back 4 minutes?"* **Jump back** returns to the last moment you touched the player.
- The [Journal's Diary](journal.md#drift-offs) shows it under that listening.

## Starting a timer automatically at night

Turn on **Auto sleep timer**, in the sleep timer or in [Settings](settings.md#sleep). In Settings you choose the hours and the kind of timer. Any book you start inside those hours then gets a timer.

It never adds a second timer or replaces one you set. If you turn a timer off, no automatic one comes back for that book until you next start the app.

The automatic timer never starts while your phone is connected to [CarPlay or Android Auto](in-the-car.md).
